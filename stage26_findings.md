## 2. Two general elementary facts

**Pair-switching obstruction.** If A contains k disjoint two-point pairs
with each pair inside a coset of a subgroup J, then 2^k<=|J|. Indeed,
choose one point from each pair. All 2^k distinct squarefree k-subsets
have sums in one fixed J-coset. Two equal sums would be a forbidden
squarefree replacement, after adding the complementary points of A.

**Two-prime unit-difference lemma.** If a set generates a cyclic
difference subgroup having only two distinct prime factors p,q, then
some actual difference is nonzero modulo BOTH p and q, hence is a unit.
Otherwise every pair of projected points in Z/p x Z/q agrees in a
coordinate. Choose two with different first coordinates (possible by
generation); their second coordinates must agree. Any third point with
a different second coordinate would have to agree with BOTH distinct
first coordinates, impossible. Thus the second coordinate is constant,
contradicting generation. This applies to prime powers as well.

For a projection H->Q, let c_x count A-points in the coset x and w(t)
count ACTUAL differences projecting to t. Difference uniqueness gives

    w(0)=sum_x c_x(c_x-1),
    w(t)=sum_x c_x*c_(x-t), t!=0.

In particular two occupied different cosets of sizes u,v contribute
at least uv to one projected difference multiplicity. The orbit unions
below determine the exact weights; they do not count differences with
replacement or ignore kernel multiplicities.

## 5. Order 195

Project onto Z/13; its kernel J has order 15. Every eligible non-kernel
orbit has size 12 or 24, projecting once or twice onto EACH of the
12 nonzero residues. Within J the eligible orbit sizes are 8 (all units
of J) and 4 (order five); the order-three orbit is forbidden.

Total size 56 forces the orbit of size eight, forbids the size-four
orbit, and leaves size 48 outside J: reduction modulo 12 makes this
immediate. Consequently every surviving union has

    w(0)=8; w(t)=4 for all nonzero t in Z/13.

The independent enumeration verifies these weights for ALL 65 unions.
The occupancy equation sum c_x(c_x-1)=8 allows only four size-two
cosets, or one size-three and one size-two coset with three singletons.
No larger coset is possible, since size four already contributes 12.
The 3+2 possibility contributes at least six to a nonzero w(t), exceeding
four. Therefore there are four pairs in four cosets of J. Their 16
squarefree four-point switches cannot fit into a coset of size 15.

## 7. Order 221: an eight-term replacement

Project onto Z/13, with kernel J of order 17. Every union is one unit
orbit of size 48 plus one order-17 orbit of size eight. The unit orbit
projects four times onto every nonzero residue of Z/13. Thus again

    w(0)=8; w(t)=4 for all nonzero t.

The occupancy argument from order 195 gives four pairs in four cosets.
Let D be their four projected positions. For each ordered pair of distinct
cosets there are four differences. Since every nonzero projected residue
has weight four, the 12 ordered differences of D are all distinct:
D is a (13,4,1) perfect difference set.

**Finite lemma:** the centroid z=(sum D)/4 belongs to D. Here 4 is
invertible modulo 13. Normalize two different D-points to 0,1. Checking
the 55 choices for the other two points gives exactly:

|Normalized D|Centroid|
|---|---:|
|0,1,3,9|0|
|0,1,4,6|6|
|0,1,5,11|1|
|0,1,8,10|8|

The independent implementation does not rely on this normalization:
it checks all 715 four-subsets of Z/13, finds 52 perfect difference
sets, and verifies that EACH contains its centroid. This finite lemma
is a new explicit computation dependency, not an unproved classification.

Write each original pair as {a_i,a_i+delta_i}. Their eight signed
differences exhaust one signed doubling orbit in J, of size eight.
After unit scaling in J and freely orienting the pairs, the steps are
1,2,4,8, and the centroid pair can have step one. Multiplying by a power
of two permutes the four signed classes, so this normalization covers
whichever pair lies over z. Pair orientation changes only the constant
offset of a collection of term sums, not its residue coverage.

Choose FIVE terms from the pair over z and ONE term from each other
pair. This gives eight-term multisets with projected sum

    5z+sum_(x in D, x!=z)x =8z =2 sum D,

the projection of the all-ones target. Their kernel sums, apart from
one constant offset, range over

    {0,1,2,3,4,5} + {0,2} + {0,4} + {0,8}.

The last three sets sum to {0,2,4,...,14}; adding the interval gives
ALL integers 0,...,19, hence every residue modulo 17. Thus some such
multiset has exactly the target sum in H. Its coset counts are 5,1,1,1
instead of 2,2,2,2, so it is a distinct eight-term rival. Contradiction.
The independent result saves an explicit choice for every mod-17 residue.
