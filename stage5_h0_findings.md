# Stage 5: attempting h=0 implies D=0

## Objective and status

The active objective is to prove the diagnostic lemma:

    For every valid non-antipodal eight-element cyclic set, h=0 => D=0.

Here Delta is the set of 56 ordered nonzero differences,
h=(1/2)|{delta in Delta: 2delta not in Delta}|, and D is the number of
quartic identifications not generated by adding a point to a cubic
equality, as defined in stage4_lemma_findings.md.

THIS GENERAL LEMMA REMAINS UNPROVED. The results below do not replace
its full scope with the binary family or with two checked moduli.
There is no verified counterexample to it. The goal remains active.
No literature novelty is asserted.

Continuation: stage6_h0_findings.md now proves the ENTIRE even
difference-subgroup branch, by forcing the maximal seven-DS-pair case.
It also excludes prime difference-subgroup order in the remaining odd
branch. The full diagnostic lemma is still unproved.

## 1. An exact reduction when doubling is injective on Delta

**Proposition 1.** If h=0 and doubling is injective on Delta, the subgroup
H generated by differences of A has odd order. After translating one
point to zero, A lies in H and remains valid there.

Proof. The finite set Delta is invariant under doubling: 2Delta=Delta.
Every delta is therefore on a finite doubling cycle, so
(2^k-1)delta=0 for some k>=1. Its order is odd. The subgroup generated
by elements of odd order in a cyclic group has odd order. Translating
A into H preserves equality of equal-length multiset sums, and
equalities inside H are precisely their equalities in the ambient
group. QED.

In particular this applies at odd modulus. At even modulus h=0 by
itself does NOT imply injectivity or equality 2Delta=Delta. The
noninjective case is still part of the objective, not an excluded case.

**Proposition 1b.** Under Proposition 1, for every a in A the seven
singleton-label pairs {a,r}, r!=a, correspond to center edges forming
an undirected spanning tree on A. This does NOT require R3=0.

Proof. Doubling permutes Delta, so the map from unordered center pairs
to unordered singleton-label pairs is a bijection. Orient the edge
for {a,r} as d->e with 2d+a=2e+r, and label it r. These are seven
distinct center edges with seven distinct labels, none equal to a.
If the center edges had an undirected cycle, traverse it and put
eta_i=1 or -1 according to the edge orientation. Their equations give

    sum_i eta_i*(r_i-a)=0.

The resulting coefficient vector has c_(r_i)=eta_i and
c_a=-sum eta_i, with all other coefficients zero. It is nonzero,
has total coefficient sum zero, and every coefficient except c_a
is in {-1,0,1}. If c_a<=1 then 1-c is a nonnegative rival. If c_a>1
then every coefficient of c is >=-1, so 1+c is a nonnegative rival.
Both alternatives contradict validity. There is no undirected cycle.
Seven edges on eight vertices therefore form a spanning tree. QED.

Also no edge has destination a: 2d+a=2a+r would give 2d=a+r,
a forbidden squarefree-pair equality. When R3>0 the orientation may
have multiple sinks; the stronger single-root claim below requires R3=0.

## 2. Rooted trees forced by validity in the R3=0 case

Work in the odd subgroup from Proposition 1 and assume R3=0, where
R3=sum_y binomial(m_y-1,2), m_y=number of representations 2a+b=y.
Then all cubic fibers have size at most two. Since h=0, there are
exactly 28 two-element fibers and eight singleton fibers.

**Proposition 2.** The eight singleton cubic representations have the
form 2a+f(a), where f is a fixed-point-free permutation of A.
For each a in A, the paired cubic representations with singleton a
define a directed spanning tree on A, rooted at f^(-1)(a), with a as
a leaf. Every edge points toward that root.

Proof. Each unordered pair of different cubic centers collides exactly
once. For a fixed center a there are seven such collisions, using seven
different singleton labels: a repeated label would give a fiber of
size at least three. Thus one label f(a) is unpaired.

The map on ordered differences delta -> 2delta permutes Delta. Hence
the corresponding map from unordered center pairs to unordered
singleton-label pairs is a bijection. Each label b occurs in exactly
seven paired cubic representations, because it belongs to seven of
the unordered label pairs. There are eight cubic representations with
singleton b in total, so precisely one is unpaired. This makes f a
permutation.

Fix a. For every r!=a, the unique occurrence of the singleton pair
{a,r} gives a directed edge d->e with equation

    2d+a = 2e+r.                                  (1)

Use r as its edge label. All seven edge labels are distinct. The
source d is the center of a paired representation 2d+a. Thus every
vertex except g=f^(-1)(a) has outdegree one, and g has outdegree zero.

No edge can have destination e=a: equation (1) would imply
2d=a+r, a different equality between a double and a squarefree pair,
contrary to validity. A directed cycle of length k would, upon summing
(1) around it, give

    sum of its k distinct edge labels = k*a.

All those labels are different from a. Replacing that squarefree
subset by k copies of a in the all-ones multiset violates validity.
Thus there is no directed cycle. Every walk must end at the only
zero-outdegree vertex g, proving that the graph is a directed spanning
tree. Vertex a has indegree zero and cannot be its root (a root with
indegree zero would be isolated). Hence g!=a and f has no fixed point;
a has one outgoing edge and no incoming edge, so it is a leaf. QED.

This proof is genuinely global: it uses distinct labels around a cycle
to construct a forbidden squarefree replacement. It does not assign
one independent quartic collision to each missing doubled difference.

### A finite-presentation bound

**Corollary 2a.** Under Proposition 1b, the difference subgroup has order
at most 2187. Its order divides an explicitly specified odd determinant.

Proof. Fix a tree T_a and translate a to zero. Index the other seven
points by r and write b_r=r-a. For the edge d->e with label r, (1) gives

    b_r = 2(b_d-b_e), with b_a=0.

Let U have row r equal to the incidence row e_d-e_e after deleting
the column a. Its rows are the seven oriented edges of a spanning
tree, in the order prescribed by their distinct labels. Thus
(I-2U)b=0.

Every square minor of U is 0,1,or -1. To see this directly, each row
of an incidence submatrix has at most one +1 and one -1. If some row
has at most one nonzero entry, expand along it and induct. Otherwise
all rows have two opposite entries and their sums are zero, so the
determinant is zero. Permuting the rows does not change this property.

The determinant expansion by principal minors gives

    det(I-2U)=sum_(S subset {1,...,7}) (-2)^|S| det U[S,S].

The empty minor is one, so this integer is odd and nonzero. Its
absolute value is at most sum_S 2^|S|=3^7=2187. The adjugate identity
shows that det(I-2U) annihilates every b_r. These points generate the
cyclic difference subgroup, whose order therefore divides that
determinant. QED.

This bound applies to the ENTIRE injective h=0 branch, including R3>0.
It is not a bound of 2187 for arbitrary valid sets or for noninjective
h=0 sets. Validity also gives the lower bound |H|>=128.

## 3. Quartic propagation becomes paths and cycles

Continue under the hypotheses of Proposition 2. Let c2 be the number
of two-cycles of the permutation f. Let C be the number of cycle
components in the propagation graph on 260 distinct repeated quartic
multisets. Singleton graph vertices are not counted as cycles.

**Proposition 3.** Every propagation component is a path, a cycle,
or an isolated vertex. There are exactly 36 noncycle components, so

    K=36+C, and Sigma=C.

The isolated vertices are exactly the c2 multisets 2a+2b with
f(a)=b and f(b)=a.

Proof. Each repeated cubic has at most one different equal cubic.
A quartic has at most two distinct point deletions that leave a
repeated cubic. Thus its degree in the propagation graph is at most
two. Different deletions cannot give the same neighboring vertex:
that would allow canceling their two common points and obtaining a
different equality of pair sums, which is forbidden.

All pure quartics 4a have degree one, since 3a is not singleton
(f(a)!=a). Of the 56 quartics 3a+b, exactly eight have degree one:
b=f(a). Of the 168 ordinary quartics 2a+b+c, exactly 48 have degree
one: their tail contains f(a), and its other point has six choices.
The rest in these two classes have degree two.

A quartic 2a+2b has degree zero precisely when f(a)=b and f(b)=a.
Exactly 8-2c2 of this class have degree one; the others have degree
two. Therefore the graph has c2 isolated vertices and 72-2c2
degree-one vertices. Its path components with at least two vertices
number (72-2c2)/2, and its noncycle components number
c2+(72-2c2)/2=36. This proves K=36+C. Finally d=28 and
Sigma=8d-(260-K)=K-36=C. QED.

This does not prove that two different components cannot share their
quartic value. The support-separation lemma from stage 4 still applies,
but disjoint-support paths/cycles have not been ruled out.

## 4. Complete proof in the affine doubling-stable case

**Theorem 4.** Suppose A is valid, has eight distinct elements, has no
antipodal pair, and 2A=A+t for some t. Then its difference subgroup has
order 255, A is a translated binary doubling orbit in that subgroup,
and D=0. This covers all moduli, including even ambient moduli.

### Identification of the set

Put B=A-t. Then 2B=B, and doubling permutes its eight elements.
The sum of the points in any doubling cycle is zero: twice that sum
equals the same sum. Thus sum B=0.

If there were more than one cycle, select a cycle with k<8 points.
Its all-ones sum is zero. Whenever a point b is used, replace that
occurrence by two copies of its predecessor in the cycle. This
preserves the sum and increases the multiset length by one. Repeating
until length eight produces a zero-sum multiset supported on that
proper cycle, distinct from the all-ones multiset of B. This contradicts
validity. Therefore B is one cycle of length eight:

    B={b,2b,4b,...,128b}, (2^8-1)b=0.

Its generated subgroup has order dividing 255. Translate one point
to zero. The 128 distinct subset sums of the other seven points lie
in that subgroup, so its order is at least 128. The only divisor of
255 with that property is 255. Thus b generates an order-255 subgroup.

### Connectivity of every quartic fiber

Identify that subgroup with Z/255 and its points with
P={1,2,4,...,128}. Write p(c) for the predecessor of c in its doubling
cycle, so 2p(c)=c modulo 255. A carry replaces two copies of p(c)
by one c, decreasing multiset length by one.

A repeated degree-four multiset has a carry and therefore reduces to
a degree-three multiset of the same value. Repeated carries terminate
at a squarefree multiset with at most three points. Such a multiset
has integer sum in [1,224], and its ordinary binary expansion determines
it uniquely. In particular its value is nonzero and its number of
points is uniquely determined.

All degree-four expansions of a fixed degree-three multiset C lie in
one propagation component. Indeed, expanding distinct occurrences
c,d in C gives

    2p(c)+d+e, and c+2p(d)+e,

where e is the remaining occurrence. Their equality is the repeated
cubic equality 2p(c)+d=c+2p(d), with e added. Expanding identical
occurrences gives the same vertex.

If a quartic value has a degree-three squarefree representation, that
representation is unique: a repeated triple would carry to fewer than
three binary digits. Therefore all its quartic vertices are expansions
of the same C and are connected.

Otherwise all its degree-three representations are repeated and carry
to degree two. Degree-two representations of any value in P are
unique. A squarefree pair gives exactly its two binary digits; a double
gives exactly the single next digit, with a unique predecessor. Thus
all the triples are expansions of the same degree-two multiset D2.
If D2 is a double, there is only one such expansion. If D2 has distinct
points c,d, the two possible triples are

    2p(c)+d, and c+2p(d).

The quartic 2p(c)+2p(d) carries to both of these triples, connecting
their expansion components. Every repeated quartic vertex carries
to one of the triples considered. Hence every quartic value fiber is
connected and D=0. Translation and subgroup identification preserve
these propagation edges, completing the proof. QED.

The missing implication is NOT being assumed: h=0 has not been shown
to force 2A=A+t for a valid eight-element set. Theorem 4 proves a
sufficient structural case, not the objective in full.

**Corollary 4a.** Under Proposition 2, if at least one of the rooted
trees is a root-centered star, then D=0. The previous wording "star"
was ambiguous about the root: the proof here covers the root-centered
case only. Stage 6 separately excludes a star rooted at a leaf.

Proof. Let T_a be a star with root g. Every source d!=g has an edge
d->g, and the seven labels r run through A without a. Equation (1)
gives 2d=r+(2g-a). The remaining point d=g satisfies
2g=a+(2g-a). Thus 2A=A+(2g-a), so Theorem 4 applies. QED.

## 5. A valid noninjective h=0 family

**Theorem 5.** The set

    N=254, A={0,1,129,131,135,143,159,191}

is valid, non-antipodal, and has h=0 but |2Delta|=42<56. It is NOT
affine doubling-stable. Nevertheless D=0, |T3|=36, |T4|=92, and
|E4|=63. In particular the stronger structural assertion
h=0 => 2A=A+t is false even with full validity.

Proof. Use the Chinese remainder identification Z/254=Z/2 x Z/127.
The set becomes {z} union B, where z=(0,0) and

    B={(1,c): c in C}, C={1,2,4,8,16,32,64} in Z/127.

The target is (1,0). A multiset of length eight with that target has
an odd number k of B terms, hence k in {1,3,5,7}. For k<7, a sum of
k points of C cannot vanish. Cyclic binary carries decrease length
until the remaining points are distinct; at most six distinct binary
digits have sum strictly between 0 and 127. If k=7 and a point is
repeated, a carry again leaves length <=6 and cannot vanish. Thus a
zero-sum length-seven multiset from C must use every point once.
The only length-eight target representation in A is therefore z once
and every point of B once. This proves validity.

Differences inside B have even first coordinate and 42 distinct
nonzero second coordinates, namely Delta_C. Cross differences are
the 14 points (1,+/-c), c in C. They are distinct because C and -C
are disjoint (doubling has odd order seven on C). There is no
antipodal difference (1,0), so A is non-antipodal.

Doubling preserves Delta_C. It sends cross differences to
(0,+/-2c), which lie in Delta_C: C=2C and c=2c-c is a difference
between two distinct points of C. Thus h=0 and 2Delta is precisely
the 42 within-B differences. Every point of 2A has even parity,
whereas any translate of A has a 7/1 split between the parity classes.
So 2A is not a translate of A.

The cubic set has eight even-parity values {0} union C, and 28
odd-parity values C+C. All pair sums from C are distinct, as follows
from their binary carries: a double gives one uniquely determined
digit, and a squarefree pair gives two uniquely determined digits.
Also C is contained in C+C. Hence |T3|=8+28=36 and R3=0.

For quartics, the odd-parity repeated representations have either
one B term and three z's or three B terms and one z. ALL contain z,
so their fibers are connected by cubic propagation after deleting z.

The even-parity repeated representations have zero, two, or four B
terms. Value zero contains only 4z, because fewer than seven terms
of C cannot vanish. Four-B repeated fibers are connected by exactly
the carry argument in Theorem 4: with seven binary digits instead
of eight, the same degree-four and degree-three argument applies.
At a value with a two-B representation 2z+c+d, the pair c+d in C
is unique. Expand c by two inverse carries into the repeated triple

    R(c)=2p(p(c))+p(c), whose sum is c.

The cubic equality 2z+c=R(c) holds in Z/2 x Z/127. Adding d connects
the two-B vertex to a four-B repeated vertex. Thus every even-parity
fiber is connected too, proving D=0.

The odd-parity quartic values are C+C, of cardinality 28. The
even-parity values are {0} union (C+C+C). A triple sum from C has
one, two, or three binary digits, and every such digit pattern is
represented by a triple, using inverse carries when necessary.
There are 7+21+35=63 nonzero triple values. Therefore |T4|=64+28=92,
and |E4|=92-29=63. QED.

### Classification of the maximal double-squarefree antipodal case

**Corollary 5a.** Any valid non-antipodal eight-element set whose pair
sum set has seven double-squarefree antipodal pairs is equivalent,
inside its difference subgroup, to Theorem 5. That subgroup has order
254, and D=0.

Proof. Write t for the involution. A double-squarefree antipodal
relation is 2b-h-l=t. Its two tail points differ from b, since a
coinciding tail would give an antipodal pair in A. Distinct such tail
edges must intersect: otherwise adding their equations gives a
nonzero zero-sum vector with all negative coefficients >=-1, producing
a rival. A pairwise-intersecting family of seven distinct edges is
a star (a nonstar intersecting family has at most the three edges of
a triangle). Translate its hub h to zero.

The seven doubled centers are distinct because each pair sum has at
most one antipode. The hub cannot be a center, so the centers are
all seven other points B. The seven star leaves are likewise all B.
Consequently 2B=B+t.

The differences of B are invariant under doubling and generate an
odd subgroup H. Modulo H, B is one coset u; the identity 2B=B+t
gives u=t. Hence C=B-t is contained in H and satisfies 2C=C.
The subset B of a valid set is itself valid for length seven;
translation gives the same property for C.

The cycle-refinement proof from Theorem 4 shows that C must be one
doubling cycle of length seven. A generator of that cycle has order
dividing 2^7-1=127, and has nontrivial order. Since 127 is prime,
H has order 127. Together H and the involution t generate an
order-254 subgroup, and A={0} union (t+C) is exactly the family in
Theorem 5. Its propagation conclusion is therefore D=0. QED.

This covers the MAXIMAL seven-pair case, not all noninjective h=0
configurations with fewer antipodal pairs in the pair-sum set.

## 6. Why a homometry shortcut is insufficient

In odd modulus h=0 makes A and 2A homometric, because their sets of
56 ordered differences coincide. Homometry alone does not imply
2A is a translate or reflection of A in a cyclic group.

A concrete Sidon example is

    N=57, A={0,1,3,13,32,36,43,52}.

Its 28 unordered circular distances, listed by successive first point,
are

    1,3,13,25,21,14,5;
    2,12,26,22,15,6;
    10,28,24,17,8;
    19,23,27,18;
    4,11,20;
    7,16;
    9.

These are exactly 1 through 28, so Delta is every nonzero residue and
h=0. But A has a cyclic gap of length 19, whereas 2A has largest cyclic
gap 18; no translation or reflection identifies them. This is NOT
valid: the baseline sum is 180=9 mod 57, as is the different eight-term
multiset {0,0,0,0,0,1,13,52}, whose sum is 66. Also its required
128 distinct translated subset sums cannot fit in modulus 57.
Thus any structural proof must use full validity,
not just difference uniqueness and doubling closure.

Results about real-line collision-free reconstruction cannot simply
be applied in the cyclic setting. The cyclic distinction is discussed
in the primary-source classification
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0012365X26004243.
No reconstruction theorem from that paper is used here.

## 7. Precisely bounded computational checks

stage5_h0_probe.py performs two CONDITIONAL configuration searches,
at N=255 and 273 only. It does not scan all moduli. For each fixed odd
N, a symmetric h=0 difference set is a union of joint doubling/negation
orbits. The script enumerates their unions of cardinality 56.

If such a difference set contains a unit, any realizing set uses that
unit as an actual difference, because its 56 differences fill the
56-element candidate. Translation and unit scaling therefore normalize
the set to contain 0,1. It suffices to examine all candidate masks
containing 1. Unit-free candidates retain only the translation 0;
there is no unsupported unit-difference assumption for those cases.

An eight-element clique with 56 unique differences in a candidate mask
uses the mask exactly. The search checks hereditary multiset validity
through the exact extension criterion from stage3_search.py, and every
accepted set is checked through all 6435 eight-term multisets.
That extension encoding was rerun against 766 small direct comparisons
and three known eight-element examples in this stage.

| N | All orbit-union masks | Normalized masks completed | Visited valid prefixes, sizes 6,7,8 | Valid affine classes |
|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|
|273|55|20|0,0,0|0|
|255|1183|322|12,8,4|1|

Both fixed 20-second caps were respected and both searches completed,
in about 0.39 and 2.56 seconds. The one affine class at 255 is binary;
the retained representative is {0,1,9,73,75,91,219,223}, with
T3=36,T4=92,R3=0,D=0. Four accepted normalized sets are NOT four classes.
There are no valid sets at 273 within the h=0 scope; this is not an
exclusion of all valid eight-element sets at 273.

stage5_h0_independent.py uses separate orbit generation and direct
multiset enumeration for prefix validity, importing no search helpers.
Both its searches completed, within separate 25-second caps: about
1.75 seconds at 273 and 13.27 seconds at 255. It independently reproduced
all mask counts and the final accepted affine classes, with the binary
key {0,1,3,7,15,31,63,127} at 255. Intermediate visited-prefix counts
differ because the original search anticipates insufficient legal
extensions; neither is a census of all valid smaller sets.

stage5_structural_checks.py directly checks all 6435 eight-term multisets
for three examples: the ordinary binary set at 255, the accepted
representative at 255, and the binary embedding at even ambient modulus
510. It constructs the rooted trees and quartic support-overlap graphs
without importing search or propagation code. The machine-readable
results are in stage5_structural_results.json. These small checks support
the proofs' bookkeeping, not their universal hypotheses.

Reproduce with standard-library Python 3:

    python stage5_h0_probe.py
    python stage5_h0_independent.py
    python stage5_structural_checks.py

These finite checks do not establish the objective over all moduli.

## 8. Exact unresolved cases and next proof obligation

The full h=0 => D=0 objective still needs one of the following:

* A proof of affine doubling stability in an appropriate injective
  branch, followed by Theorem 4. Theorem 5 REFUTES that implication
  for the full objective, so it cannot be the universal route.
* A direct proof that two disjoint-support propagation components
  cannot share a quartic value, without classifying A.

In the injective R3=0 case, Propositions 2-3 supply an oriented
double cover of the complete center graph by eight rooted trees,
plus the path/cycle structure of the quartic graph. They do NOT yet
exclude equality between different components. Nor do they prove that
the eight trees are stars, although they are stars in the binary case.
When R3>0, Proposition 1b and the determinant bound still apply, but
the rooted orientation and quartic path/cycle count do not. The
noninjective branch was covered only in Corollary 5a's maximal case at
the end of Stage 5. Stage 6 now proves that every noninjective h=0
instance is in that maximal case; see stage6_h0_findings.md.

The finite-presentation bound suggests a targeted next action in the
injective branch: classify nonstar tree relation templates
(I-2U)b=0 and their cyclic quotients, retaining the full validity and
h=0 checks. This derives candidate groups from relation matrices rather
than sweeping moduli. Showing that some tree must be a star would
suffice, but is stronger than D=0 and is not assumed necessary.

No proof of the original 248 bound or of the full diagnostic lemma
is claimed. No PR is available because this workspace has no Git
repository or configured GitHub remote. The active goal is not complete.
