# Stage 6: the even branch of h=0 implies D=0

## Status

The objective remains: every valid non-antipodal eight-element cyclic
set with h=0 has D=0. It is NOT proved in full. This stage proves its
entire even-difference-subgroup branch, not just the seven-DS-pair
special case. The remaining subgroup is odd, composite, and at most
2187. No novelty claim is made.

Continuation: stage7_h0_findings.md excludes odd prime powers and
negative doubling stability, and proves D_(31/31)<=2R3. That loss
vanishes at R3=0 but does not control all of D. The goal is still open.

Here Delta consists of the 56 ordered nonzero differences, h counts
unordered differences whose doubles leave Delta, and D is the number
of quartic identifications not generated by coning repeated cubic
equalities. The definition uses 260 distinct repeated quartic multisets,
not 288 marks. Write H for the cyclic subgroup generated by differences.
Translation puts A in H and preserves validity, h, and D.

## 1. Complete classification when H has even order

**Theorem.** If A is valid, non-antipodal, has eight elements, h=0,
and |H| is even, then |H|=254. Up to translation and an automorphism
of H, the set is

    {0,1,129,131,135,143,159,191} in Z/254.

Every quartic fiber is connected by cubic propagation: D=0. Also
|T3|=36, |T4|=92, and |E4|=63. In particular this branch is impossible
at ambient modulus N<248.

### Prerequisites and the loss formula

If a nonzero integer relation c on A has total coefficient sum zero
and weighted sum zero, it must have a coefficient at least 2 AND a
coefficient at most -2. Otherwise either 1-c or 1+c is a nonnegative
eight-term rival for the all-ones multiset. Consequently no squarefree
multiset of length at most eight equals a different multiset of the
same length. The no-antipode condition then implies uniqueness of all
36 two-term multiset sums and all 56 ordered nonzero differences.

Work inside H, write t=|H|/2, and let P be the 36 pair sums. Classify
the antipodal pairs in P as DS (double-squarefree), SS
(squarefree-squarefree), or DD (double-double), with counts d,e,j.
Members of an antipodal pair cannot share a point: cancellation would
give an antipodal pair in A.

An antipodal pair of differences a-b and c-d gives the antipodal pair
of sums a+d and b+c. Conversely, a DS antipodal pair gives exactly
two unordered antipodal difference pairs, an SS pair gives four, and
a DD pair gives one. These correspond to the two or four ways of
matching the distinct points across the two pair sums, identifying
matching order and simultaneous reversal where appropriate. Uniqueness
of ordered differences makes these pairs distinct and prevents any
pair from being counted for two different antipodal pairs of sums.
Since doubling has fibers of size at most two,

    |2Delta| = 56 - 2d - 4e - j.                 (1)

Under h=0, j=0: a DD relation 2a-2b=t would send the difference a-b
to t, which is not in Delta because A has no antipodes.

The following bounds are elementary; no seven-element computational
certification is used.

* DS tails intersect pairwise. Indeed, adding two relations
  2a-b-c=t with disjoint tails gives a nonzero zero-sum relation whose
  negative coefficients are all at least -1, contradicting validity.
  Distinct tail edges thus form a star or a triangle. In a star the
  hub cannot be a doubled center, and the centers are distinct, so
  d<=7; a triangle has at most three edges.
* e<=1. For two different SS relations r,r', both r+r' and r-r'
  are nonzero zero-sum relations. To have coefficients of both signs
  of magnitude at least two, all four intersections between their
  positive and negative support pairs must be nonempty. Thus both
  use the same four points and the second partition crosses the first.
  For example, subtracting u+v-w-z=t and u+w-v-z=t gives 2(v-w)=0,
  an excluded antipodal pair in A. The other crossing is identical.
* If e=1, each DS tail must meet both halves of its SS partition:
  compare the DS relation with both signs of the SS relation; each
  resulting zero-sum relation needs a negative coefficient at most -2.
  The tails are therefore pairwise-intersecting cross edges in K_(2,2),
  of which there can be at most two. Hence d<=2 in this case.

It follows from (1) that

    56-|2Delta| <=14,

and equality forces d=7 and e=j=0. With e=1 the loss is at most eight.

### Parity forces the extremal loss

Translate so 0 is in A and A generates H. Let p and q be the numbers
of its even and odd points, respectively. Both are positive: if all
points were even, A would generate a proper subgroup. Also p+q=8.
Exactly 2pq ordered differences are odd, so there are 56-2pq even
differences. Since h=0, every doubled difference belongs to Delta;
all those doubled differences are even. Thus

    56-14 <= |2Delta| <= 56-2pq,
    pq <=7.

For positive p,q with p+q=8, this is possible only for {p,q}={1,7},
where pq=7. Both inequalities are equalities. Hence d=7, e=j=0.
This is the additional step that settles the full even branch.

### Identification of A

The seven DS tail edges form a star. Translate its hub to zero and
call the other seven points B. Its seven centers are distinct and
different from the hub; its seven leaves are also all B. The DS
relations therefore give

    2B=B+t.                                     (2)

Let H0 be the subgroup generated by differences of B. Equation (2)
implies 2Delta_B=Delta_B, with doubling injective there. Every such
difference is periodic under doubling, hence has odd order; H0 is odd.
All B points have one coset u modulo H0, and (2) gives 2u=u+t.
Thus C=B-t lies in H0 and satisfies 2C=C.

Validity is hereditary: a seven-term rival for B can be padded with
the omitted point to give an eight-term rival for A. Translation then
makes C valid for length seven. Doubling permutes C. Each doubling
cycle has sum zero, because twice its sum equals its sum. If there
were multiple cycles, choose one proper cycle and repeatedly replace
an occurrence by two copies of its predecessor. This preserves its
zero sum and increases length, reaching a seven-term zero-sum rival
supported on that proper cycle. Therefore C is one cycle of length seven.

For a point c in that cycle, 127c=0 and c!=0. Since 127 is prime,
H0 has order 127. Together with t it generates H of order 254.
In Z/2 x Z/127, A is precisely

    {z} union {(1,c): c in {1,2,4,8,16,32,64}}, z=(0,0).

An automorphism sends the generator c to 1. This gives the stated set.

### Validity and quartic connectivity of the classified family

Let C be the seven binary digits above, and p(c) its cyclic predecessor.
A binary carry replaces two p(c)'s by c. Repeated carries strictly
decrease length, ending at a nonempty squarefree digit set. With at
most six digits, its integer sum lies strictly between 0 and 127.
Thus a zero sum using at most seven digits must use all seven once.
The eight-term target of A is (1,0), so an alternative would have an
odd number 1,3,5,or 7 of B terms. The carry observation excludes every
alternative. Also t=(1,0) is not a difference, so A is non-antipodal.

For quartic connectivity, distinguish the parity of the number of B
terms. Odd-parity repeated quartics have one or three B terms and
all contain z. Deleting z leaves repeated cubics, so each such fiber
is connected by cubic propagation.

Even-parity repeated quartics have zero, two, or four B terms. Value
zero contains only 4z. Four-B repeated quartics have connected fibers:
carry to a triple; all expansions of a fixed triple are connected by
the cubic exchange

    2p(c)+d = c+2p(d),

coned with its third occurrence. A squarefree triple normal form is
unique. Otherwise the triples reduce to the unique pair normal form;
if that pair has two distinct points c,d, the common quartic expansion
2p(c)+2p(d) joins its two triple expansions. If it is a double there is
only one triple expansion. These claims follow from ordinary binary
uniqueness: at most three distinct digits have sum below 127, and a
pair's carry normal form uniquely determines the pair. Carries themselves
are NOT asserted to be propagation edges; the displayed exchanges are.

A two-B quartic is 2z+c+d with its pair c,d unique. The equality

    2z+c = 2p(p(c))+p(c)

is a repeated cubic equality in Z/2 x Z/127. Coning by d joins the
two-B vertex to its four-B component. Every quartic fiber is connected,
so D=0.

The cubic values number 8 in even parity and 28 in odd parity. The
quartic values number 64 in even parity (zero plus all one-, two-, and
three-digit patterns) and 28 in odd parity (all one- and two-digit
patterns). Hence T3=36,T4=92,E4=92-29=63. This completes the theorem.

## 2. The remaining subgroup cannot be prime

In odd H, h=0 implies 2Delta=Delta. The audited spanning-tree proof
and determinant bound in stage5_h0_findings.md apply with or without
R3=0: after fixing a base, a seven-label tree gives

    (I-2U)b=0,  |H| divides det(I-2U),  |H|<=3^7=2187.

All incidence minors are 0,+1,-1, so the principal-minor expansion
proves the bound, and the determinant is nonzero because it is odd.
The 128 distinct translated subset sums give |H|>=128.

If |H|=p were prime, the group generated by 2 and -1 in (Z/p)^*
would act freely on every nonzero difference. Delta is a union of
its equal-size orbits, so their size divides 56. In particular
ord_p(2) divides 56, and p divides 2^56-1. The exact factorization is

    2^56-1 = 3*5*17*29*43*113*127*15790321.

The last factor P is prime. Here is a small explicit certificate,
not a probabilistic primality test. Set F=3*7*13*241=65793>sqrt(P),
with F dividing P-1. The four factors of F are prime. Modular powers
give 3^(P-1)=1 modulo P and the following values:

| q | 3^((P-1)/q) mod P | gcd(value-1,P) |
|---:|---:|---:|
|3|14592969|1|
|7|15790305|1|
|13|13946016|1|
|241|2286503|1|

For any prime divisor r of P, these identities force the order of 3
modulo r to be divisible by each q, hence F divides r-1. Thus every
prime divisor r is greater than sqrt(P), proving P prime. None of
the displayed prime factors belongs to [128,2187], a contradiction.

Consequently every remaining h=0 instance has ODD COMPOSITE difference
subgroup order between 128 and 2187. The even case has order exactly 254.
For ambient N<248 the subset cube also forces H to be the whole group:
a proper subgroup has at most N/2<128 points. Thus in that range h=0
requires N odd and composite.

## 3. A rigorous overlap restriction for the rooted trees

**Lemma.** Two homometric Sidon sets X,Y of the same size at least five
in an odd cyclic group cannot differ in just one point. Here Sidon
means that all two-term multiset sums are unique; homometric means
that their ordered difference multiplicities agree.

Proof. Suppose X=B union {x}, Y=B union {y}, x!=y. Write B also for
its indicator in the integer group ring, B^- for the reflected
indicator, and e_v for the basis element at v. Equality of
autocorrelations, after canceling B*B^- and the common constant term,
gives

    (e_x-e_y)*(B^- - e_(-x-y)*B)=0.

Thus J=B-e_(x+y)*B^- is invariant under translation by x-y. Its
coefficients are in {-1,0,1}. A nonzero coefficient would force an
entire coset of the nontrivial odd-order subgroup <x-y> into B or its
reflection. Such a coset has at least three points and repeats an
ordered nonzero difference, contradicting Sidon uniqueness. Hence
J=0: B is invariant under reflection b -> x+y-b.

In an odd group this reflection has only one fixed point. Since
|B|>=4, B has at least two distinct two-point reflection orbits.
Their squarefree pair sums both equal x+y, again contradicting Sidon.
This proves the lemma. QED.

**Corollary.** In the odd h=0 branch, if a singleton-label tree T_a has
a vertex v of indegree at least six, A is affine doubling-stable,
and D=0. This does NOT require R3=0. Consequently every tree not
oriented as a root-centered star has indegree at most five. In the
R3=0 branch, an undirected star rooted at a leaf is impossible.

Proof. Each incoming edge d->v gives 2d+a=2v+r with distinct sources
d and distinct labels r!=a. Hence 2A and A+(2v-a) have at least six
common points from those equations, and also share 2v, giving at
least seven common points. These two eight-point sets are Sidon and
homometric because 2Delta=Delta. If they differ in one point the
lemma contradicts them, so they are equal. The affine-stability
theorem then identifies A with the binary orbit at 255. Its cubic
pairings make every T_a a root-centered star. A star rooted at a
leaf has indegree six at its center, so cannot be a different case.
QED.

This repairs an ambiguity in the old star argument: a rooted tree's
underlying star need not be centered at its root. The old direct
argument covered only the root-centered case; the overlap lemma
now excludes the other rooted orientation. None of this excludes all
nonstar trees or settles R3>0. The indegree-six restriction itself
does not require R3=0: incoming edges in a simple tree have distinct
sources, and the seven singleton labels are distinct in Proposition 1b.

## 4. Relation-derived template checks

These checks do not scan moduli. They enumerate explicit seven-edge
relation matrices and only their cyclic quotients. In the odd R3=0
branch, every base has a directed tree toward one root, and the base
is a leaf different from that root. Paths and diameter-three double
stars are two complete, bounded families of such rooted templates.

Each template assigns labels 1,...,7 bijectively to its edges. A label
equal to its edge's source is rejected: its relation would equate a
double to the squarefree pair consisting of the source and base.

Integer row and column operations give L M V=diag(d_i), with L,V
unimodular. The solutions modulo m are exactly b=V y, where d_i*y_i=0.
Only m dividing lcm(|d_i|) can be generated by a primitive solution.
All such m>=128 and all primitive solution orbits are included.
Unit normalization is done separately at every prime power by making
the first unit coordinate one, then combined by CRT. No assumption
of a unit difference in A is used. If the presentation group is cyclic,
its surjective maps to a fixed cyclic quotient are one unit orbit.

| Family | Label permutations | Admissible templates | Cyclic quotient maps up to units | Distinct eight-point outputs | Sidon outputs | h=0 outputs |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
|Rooted paths|35280|14833|15380|14109|10515|0|
|Rooted double stars|95760|40261|30310|8608|6796|0|

The path family used all seven root positions. The double-star family
used all five possible numbers of leaves on the marked-base side,
and all root orbits under automorphisms fixing that base: 19 rooted
shape representatives. All edge-label permutations were included.
Outputs were deduplicated by identical sorted residue sets, NOT claimed
to be distinct affine classes. Neither family produced a candidate
requiring the final full validity check, because all Sidon outputs
failed doubling closure. Every family finished: about 2.86 seconds
within a 35-second cap and 7.23 seconds within a 30-second cap.

These are computational family exclusions, not an elementary proof
that arbitrary nonstar trees are impossible. They require the rooted
R3=0 hypothesis and do not cover the R3>0 orientations.

## 5. Precise remaining obstruction

The single remaining diagnostic lemma is:

    For valid A with eight points in an odd composite cyclic group,
    128<=|H|<=2187, and 2Delta=Delta, two different cubic-propagation
    components cannot have the same repeated quartic sum.

By support separation, components at the same value have disjoint
entire supports. Neither the tree determinant bound nor exclusion of
paths and double stars proves that such components cannot occur for
other tree shapes or for R3>0. Requiring a star would be stronger than
needed; affine doubling stability is not assumed.

Even proving the full diagnostic lemma would not by itself prove the
248 bound. In the original collision accounting the separate target is

    Sigma-D >= 53-6h-6R3.

For h=0 and R3=0, D=0 still leaves Sigma>=53 to prove. Instances with
h>0 also remain outside the diagnostic lemma. The active objective is
therefore not marked complete.

All classification and prime-order arguments here are self-contained
elementary mathematics plus the displayed fixed-integer arithmetic.
The template exclusions are explicitly computational. No result relies
on the paper's n<=7 certification; no literature novelty is asserted.
